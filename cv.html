Research profile

I am an environmental macroeconomist developing a research agenda on how growth, technological change, and public policy interact under multiple environmental constraints. My work combines dynamic general equilibrium modelling, mathematical analysis, and numerical simulation with firm- and city-level policy evaluation.

Research fields: environmental macroeconomics; directed technical change; endogenous growth; climate and environmental policy; circular economy and recycling; innovation and AI diffusion.

Doctoral dissertation

The Multi-Subsystem Environmental Constraints and Endogenous Economic Growth: A Directed Technical Change Approach

Doctoral dissertation, Nankai University, 2026. Sole author.

Develops a dynamic general equilibrium model of directed technical change with multiple environmental subsystems, clean-technology non-carbon burdens, nonlinear environmental damage, and regeneration capacity that deteriorates as pressure accumulates. The analysis covers decentralised and planner allocations, implementation policy, transition dynamics, cycles, and sensitivity.
